# Safe Crewing For Maritime SAR Operations - GU1007 Notes

Restricted internal reference summary for Launch Ready.

Source:
- Safe Crewing for Maritime SAR Operations Guidance - GU1007.pdf
- Published: 20/03/2026
- Classification: Restricted

## Core Purpose

- The guidance sets the minimum and maximum crew numbers and the minimum role complement needed to safely operate RNLI SAR assets.
- It applies to service launches, exercise launches, passages, and boat movements.
- It is intended to help launch authorities make informed decisions about whether an asset can safely go, should be delayed, or should be off service.

## Crewing Numbers

- Severn / Tamar / Trent:
  - Service and exercise minimum: 5
  - Maximum on service, exercise, and passage: 7
  - Passage minimum: 4
- Shannon:
  - Service and exercise minimum: 5
  - Maximum on service, exercise, and passage: 6
  - Passage minimum: 4
- B class / E class:
  - Service and exercise minimum: 3
  - Maximum on service, exercise, and passage: 4
  - Passage minimum: 2
  - Passage minimum in darkness: 3
- D class / Hovercraft:
  - Service and exercise minimum: 3
  - Maximum on service, exercise, and passage: 4
  - Passage minimum: 3
- A class lifeboat use:
  - Service and exercise minimum: 2
  - Maximum on service, exercise, and passage: 3
  - Passage minimum: 2
- RWC lifeboat use:
  - Service and exercise minimum: 2
  - Maximum on service, exercise, and passage: 2
  - Passage minimum: 2
- Daughter craft:
  - Service and exercise minimum: 2
  - Maximum on service, exercise, and passage: 2
  - Passage minimum: not applicable

## Minimum Role Complement

- The guidance requires a minimum crew complement by role, not just by headcount.
- Some roles must be filled by fully competent and current crew before launch.
- The person in command must remain onboard while the asset is in operation, except in abandon-ship scenarios.
- For many assets, a navigator is expected to be on board or is becoming a future requirement.
- Launch Ready should treat these role requirements as hard-stop readiness constraints when they are designated as such.

## Service, Exercise, And Passage Implications

- On exercise, some role requirements may be relaxed with launch-authority discretion if mitigations are in place.
- Those mitigations can include non-SAR-capable exercise status, planning for weather and recovery conditions, and ensuring a robust embank/disembark plan if tasking occurs.
- For service requests, the required crew complement must be met before launch unless a formal risk-managed exception process is used.
- The guidance explicitly separates the normal operational standard from exceptional or concession-based operation.

## Head Launcher Requirements

- Every launch and recovery must have a designated Head Launcher unless a documented exception applies.
- The Head Launcher must hold a completed Head Launcher development plan.
- The Head Launcher must not:
  - work in dual roles during launch or recovery
  - be part of the operating boat crew
  - act as Launch Authority for the same launch
- Exceptions may exist for:
  - boats kept afloat alongside or at moorings
  - floating dock, hydraulic lift, or ILB davit situations
  - some straightforward low-risk launches with approved local exceptions
  - some remote-site ILB setups where the vehicle driver may act as Head Launcher
- ALB carriage or slipway launches require a dedicated Head Launcher.

## Dynamic Risk Assessment Boundary

- If a sufficient number of competent and current crew are not present for a service request, the launch authority and command roles must conduct a dynamic risk assessment.
- The available options include declining the launch or delaying until suitable crew are available.
- The dynamic risk assessment cannot override the roles that are explicitly marked as non-negotiable in the guidance.
- A detailed service return must be made when the guidance cannot be met.

## Operational Implications For Launch Ready

- Readiness should be calculated by asset type and operation type.
- Minimum crew counts and role complements need separate rule tables.
- Head Launcher must be a first-class operational concept, not a free-text note.
- The system should distinguish:
  - launch-ready
  - delayed-launch possible
  - off service
  - non-SAR capable / exercise only
  - hard-stop gaps
- Any future exception workflow should require:
  - reason
  - approving authority or role
  - notes
  - audit log entry
  - service return note flag
