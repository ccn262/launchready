# Lifeboat Training Standards Handbook TP-OCF-02 Notes

Restricted internal reference summary for Launch Ready.

Source:
- Lifeboat Training Standards Handbook TP-OCF-02.pdf
- Reviewed pages: 10-40

## What The Handbook Is For

- The Operational Competence Framework is the RNLI structure for training, assessment, progression, and competence assurance.
- It is maintained centrally, reviewed continuously, and the online version is authoritative.
- Training and competence records are expected to be auditable and retained for reporting, verification, and assurance.

## Competence Model

- Competence is organised into activities, task groups, and tasks.
- The framework is task-based rather than a simple role checkbox model.
- Most tasks are not revalidated on a fixed timer, but some areas do require periodic refresh.
- The main exceptions called out are safety/health/environment tasks, emergency and survival procedures, and clinical items.

## Training Pathway Pattern

- Training plans give a structured progression for a specific role.
- The afloat progression is staged:
  - Safety and induction
  - Safe to go afloat
  - Safe to go on a service
  - Tier 1 crew
  - Tier 2 crew
- The point of the staged model is to keep people progressing in a logical order and to prevent operational use before they are ready.
- Training plans differ by SAR unit type and by shore role, so Launch Ready should treat them as role-specific and asset-specific rather than global.

## Pass Outs And Competence Gates

- A pass out is the evidence point that a trainee can combine knowledge and skill in an operational scenario.
- Being marked trained in a task means the station believes the individual can perform it to the RNLI standard, including in exercise or service contexts.
- Launch Ready should treat pass out state as a separate readiness signal from raw attendance or task completion.

## Training Methods And Evidence

- Training may be self-directed, online, one-to-one, group-based, course-based, or by shadowing and observation.
- Attendance, training events, exercises, and launches that contribute to competence are meant to be recorded.
- For Launch Ready, this supports a future workflow where training evidence, exercise participation, and currency are all visible in one place.

## Currency And Maintenance

- After Tier 1 pass out, crew begin maintaining currency through structured activity.
- Currency is not a test; it is a recurring practice model to keep skills current.
- Stations should plan enough opportunities for crew to maintain competence.
- Individuals are responsible for managing their own progress, and station leadership is responsible for monitoring progress overall.
- If crew fall behind, the framework expects early intervention, possible extra training, and eventually role reduction or stand-down if needed.

## Fitness And Medical Implications

- Physical tasks are expected to be demonstrable.
- Crew are expected to be fit enough to complete the tasks in their role without undue difficulty.
- In-date health assessment is part of the competence picture.

## Specific Operational Implications For Launch Ready

- Model training as a progression state, not just a yes/no qualification.
- Track:
  - stage completion
  - pass out status
  - currency activity status
  - overdue status
  - reassessment or stand-down recommendations
- Support per-role and per-asset training histories rather than a single global crew status.
- Treat training evidence, exercise attendance, and service participation as part of the competence record.
- Surface a clear distinction between:
  - trained
  - current
  - overdue
  - restricted
  - stood down

## Notes For Future Work

- The handbook reinforces that future readiness logic must be evidence-based and role-specific.
- It supports readiness views that include training stage, currency state, and current competence, but it does not itself authorise launches.
- This note is internal only and must not be exposed as public RNLI endorsement or approval.
